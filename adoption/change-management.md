# Change Management Approach

## Frameworks Applied

**ADKAR** (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement) is used at the individual level — for each user segment, the program explicitly plans for all five stages rather than assuming Awareness and Knowledge (communication and training) are enough on their own.

**Kotter's 8-Step Model** is used at the organizational level — particularly building the guiding coalition (sponsors + champions), generating short-term wins (the "Quick Wins" quadrant from the [Prioritization Matrix](../prioritization/prioritization-matrix.md)), and anchoring changes in how work normally gets done rather than treating adoption as a temporary campaign.

## ADKAR Applied to a New AI Use Case

| Stage | Program Action |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Communicate why this use case exists and what problem it solves |
| Desire | Show a credible, relevant example of the use case saving real time or effort |
| Knowledge | LevelUp training at the appropriate tier |
| Ability | Guided first use with a champion or job aid available |
| Reinforcement | Visible recognition of use, plus a fast path to resolve friction |

## Why Both Frameworks Together

ADKAR is strong on the individual change journey but says little about organizational momentum. Kotter is strong on organizational momentum but treats individuals as a mostly undifferentiated audience. Using both means the program plans for a single user's actual barriers to change *and* for the organizational conditions — sponsorship, short-term wins, embedded practice — that make that individual change stick.
