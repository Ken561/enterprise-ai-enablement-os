# Interview Talking Points — Enterprise AI Enablement Operating System

## 30-Second Version

"Corporate Technology was told to adopt AI, but there was no owner, no intake process, and no way to measure whether it was working. I built the operating system for that mandate — intake, tiered risk governance, a training curriculum built with HR, a champion-driven adoption model, and executive reporting — so AI adoption became a repeatable program instead of a pile of disconnected pilots."

## 2-Minute Version

**Problem →** An ambiguous, org-wide AI mandate with no operating model behind it. Teams were experimenting independently, some skipping risk review, others stuck waiting for approval that didn't exist.

**Evidence →** I ran a structured feedback program — the Listening Post Strategy — that surfaced 300+ submissions on where people were stuck and what they needed. That told me the bottleneck wasn't enthusiasm, it was the lack of a connected path from "I have an idea" to "I'm using this safely in production."

**Decision →** Instead of writing a policy document, I designed a system: intake, tiered risk review, prioritization, a tiered training curriculum (LevelUp: 101/201/301) built with HR and Talent & Development, a champion network for adoption support, and a KPI framework feeding executive reporting.

**Implementation →** I own this end to end — I'm the reference point the organization goes to for how a new AI initiative gets structured and governed. I also personally built one of the production use cases moving through this system, an agentic workflow spanning 26+ PRD sections with defined review checkpoints.

**Result →** The pattern mirrors what I've done in every technology rollout I've led — 90%+ adoption across three independent organizations — applied here to AI specifically, at enterprise scale, with governance built in from the start rather than bolted on after a problem.

## 5-Minute Version

Add to the 2-minute version:

- **Stakeholders:** Executive sponsors, engineering, security, risk/compliance, HR/Talent Development, business teams, and champions — each with defined decision rights so most requests never need to convene every group.
- **Constraints:** No formal authority over most participating teams; multiple AI tools in flight with different risk profiles; a workforce ranging from advanced developers to first-time AI users.
- **Alternatives considered:** A single uniform review process for every use case (rejected — creates a bottleneck for low-risk work); a mandate-driven rollout with no champion network (rejected — doesn't survive attention moving elsewhere, per prior rollout experience).
- **Tradeoffs:** Deliberately lighter governance on low-risk use cases in exchange for speed; one shared curriculum architecture instead of a bespoke track per role, to keep training maintainable.
- **Risks managed:** Governance tiering keeps rigor proportional to actual risk rather than uniform; human review checkpoints are required for any high-autonomy use case before launch.
- **Metrics:** A KPI framework spanning reach, activation, engagement, capability, business impact, and risk — reported monthly to the portfolio and quarterly at the strategy level.
- **Lessons / next steps:** The maturity model needs to become a recurring, investment-linked checkpoint rather than a one-time assessment; intake should move toward self-service so the program office spends less time on manual triage.

## Likely Interviewer Questions & Strong Answers

**"How is this different from just writing an AI policy?"**
A policy tells people what's allowed. This is the system that makes the policy operational — the intake point, the review path, the training that builds the skill to comply, and the reporting that shows whether it's working. Without the system, the policy is a document nobody encounters until something goes wrong.

**"How did you handle disagreement between speed and governance?"**
By tiering review to actual risk instead of treating it as a single dial. Low-risk use cases move in days through self-attestation; only use cases touching sensitive data or consequential decisions go through full governance review. That reframes the conversation from "fast vs. safe" to "which use cases actually need the slow path."

**"What would you do differently if you started over?"**
Build the self-service intake experience from day one instead of starting with manual triage. Manual triage was the right way to learn what good triage criteria actually look like, but it doesn't scale, and I'd rather have designed the self-service version once I already knew the criteria.

**"How do you know this is actually working, not just that people attended training?"**
The KPI framework deliberately leads executive reporting with business impact and risk, not attendance or activation. Training completion is treated as a leading indicator we watch, not the outcome we report as success.
