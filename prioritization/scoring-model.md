# Use-Case Scoring Model

> *Portfolio framework informed by enterprise AI program-management experience. Weights and scores below are illustrative sample data.*

## Scoring Dimensions

| Dimension | Weight | What It Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Business value | 25% | Expected impact on cost, time, quality, or revenue |
| User reach | 15% | How many people or workflows this affects |
| Feasibility | 20% | Technical and data readiness |
| Risk | 15% | Inverse score — higher risk lowers the score |
| Adoption potential | 15% | Likelihood of sustained use once launched |
| Implementation complexity | 10% | Inverse score — higher complexity lowers the score |

## Example Scoring (Sample Data)

| Use Case | Business Value | User Reach | Feasibility | Risk (inverse) | Adoption Potential | Complexity (inverse) | Weighted Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engineering Issue Capture Assistant | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | **4.30** |
| Internal Knowledge Retrieval Assistant | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | **4.35** |
| AI Meeting Summary Tool | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 4 | **3.10** |

*Scores are on a 1–5 scale, weighted and summed. Sample data for illustration only.*

## How Scores Are Used

Scoring does not make the decision automatically — it creates a ranked, defensible starting point for a prioritization conversation with sponsors. Ties or close scores are broken by strategic alignment and dependency sequencing, not by re-litigating the scoring model itself.
